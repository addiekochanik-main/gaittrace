92% accurate. Then I checked.

An AI could guess who was walking with 92.2% accuracy. But the clips overlap, so it was partly matching near-copies. When I removed the closest neighbors, accuracy fell. The chart shows how much.

A research prototype, not a diagnosis.

This uses public phone-sensor data from healthy volunteers (the UCI HAR dataset), not patients. The changes are simulated, so this does not show that the AI can detect multiple sclerosis, predict falls, or work as a medical test. The 3D view shows how similar walking clips are to each other, not real movement in space. Next step: collecting my own data with an Arduino Nano ESP32 wearable.
